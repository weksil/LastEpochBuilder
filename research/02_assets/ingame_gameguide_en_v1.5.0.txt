GameGuide.UnlockingSkills_Paragraph_0 :: Skills can be unlocked upon reaching certain character levels, after investing a certain number of passive points into a class, or after selecting a Mastery Class.
GameGuide.UnlockingSkills_Paragraph_1 :: Opening the Skills Window (bound to the S key by default) allows you to see which skills your character can unlock, and how to do so. You can also click on any skill to view its <link="skill specialization">Specialization Tree</link>.
GameGuide.UnlockingSkills_Paragraph_2 :: Clicking one of the 5 skill slots on your Action Bar at the bottom of the screen allows you to change which skill is equipped there.

GameGuide.SkillSpecialization_Title :: Skill Specialization
GameGuide.SkillSpecialization_Paragraph_0 :: Skill Specialization Slots are unlocked as your character levels up, to a maximum of 5 (to match how many skills can be equipped on the Action Bar).
GameGuide.SkillSpecialization_Paragraph_1 :: Upon unlocking a Skill Specialization Slot, you can choose to Specialize in a skill that your character currently knows. Doing so allows you to level up that skill and invest points into its Specialization Tree. Skills have a natural maximum level of 20.
GameGuide.SkillSpecialization_Paragraph_2 :: If you would like to specialize in a different skill, you can press the Respec button at the top right of the Skills Window while viewing the tree for a specialized skill.
GameGuide.SkillSpecialization_Paragraph_3 :: Newly specialized skills will be automatically set to your <#FFE6A5>Minimum Skill Level</color>, and may receive a bonus to XP so they can catch up to your other skills.
GameGuide.SkillSpecialization_Paragraph_4 :: You can see your current Minimum Skill Level on the Respec button inside a skill''s tree. Skills currently affected by a XP bonus will state so above their XP bar on the left side of their tree.

GameGuide.Implicits_Title :: Implicits
GameGuide.Implicits_Paragraph_0 :: All equipment has stats that are inherent to it, which are called implicits. For example, all Worn Boots have an increased movement speed implicit.
GameGuide.Implicits_Paragraph_2 :: Each item subtype of an item always has the same implicits, but the values of implicits are randomized within a range.
GameGuide.Implicits_Paragraph_3 :: All modifiers from implicits apply globally. For example, the “Increased Physical Damage” from a weapon’s implicit has the same effect as “Increased Physical Damage” from an <link="affixes">affix</link> on a ring or from a passive node.

GameGuide.Affixes_Title :: Affixes
GameGuide.Affixes_Paragraph_0 :: Affixes are the modifiers on equipment that exist in addition to <link="implicits">Implicits</link>. They can be present on the item when it is dropped, or they can be added and modified later through <link="Crafting Explanation">Crafting</link> using <link="shards">Shards</link>. The number of affixes on an item often determines its <link="rarity">Rarity</link>.
GameGuide.Affixes_Paragraph_2 :: Affixes have 7 tiers, with tier 1 being the lowest and tier 7 being the highest. However, only tier 1 through 5 can be created through <link="Crafting Explanation">crafting</link>. Tier 6 and 7 affixes must be on the item when it drops.
GameGuide.Affixes_Paragraph_3 :: The values of an affix at each tier fall within a range, but the lowest roll of a tier is always higher than the tier below it.<br><br>The value an affix has on an item can be re-rolled within its tier using a <link="rune of refinement">Rune of Refinement</link>.
GameGuide.Affixes_Paragraph_4 :: Affixes are broken into two main categories: prefixes and suffixes. An item can have up to 2 prefixes and 2 suffixes.
GameGuide.Affixes_Paragraph_5 :: All stats granted by affixes are global. This means the “Increased Physical Damage” affix on a ring will have the same effect as “Increased Physical Damage” on a weapon’s <link="implicit">implicit</link> or a passive node.

GameGuide.LegendaryItems_Title :: Legendary Items
GameGuide.LegendaryItems_Paragraph_0 :: A Legendary item is a composite item made up of a unique and exalted item. Unique items must have atleast 1 Legendary Potential (up to 4), and the exalted item must have exactly 4 unsealed affixes. The exalted item can have a sealed affix, it just cannot be added to the Legendary item.
GameGuide.LegendaryItems_Paragraph_2 :: When combined in the Eternity Cache, you are granted a Legendary item. The Eternity Cache is located within the Temporal Sanctum <link="Dungeons">dungeon</link>. Running higher tiers of the dungeon will allow you to create Legendaries out of higher level uniques.
GameGuide.LegendaryItems_Paragraph_4 :: The Legendary item is the same as the unique item with affixes added to it from the exalted item. The number of affixes selected is equal to the legendary potential of the unique item used. When using the Eternity Cache at the end of Tier 2 or higher Temporal Sanctum, you can choose one affix to guarantee being moved from the exalted item. If the Unique item had more than 1 Legendary Potential, the rest of the affixes are chosen at random.
GameGuide.LegendaryItems_Paragraph_6 :: The quantity of legendary potential that a unique item drops with is influenced mainly by the level of the zone which it drops in, but corruption also increases it slightly. Increased rarity mods, character level or any other modifiers have no effect.
GameGuide.LegendaryItems_Paragraph_7 :: The amount of Legendary Potential that a unique item will generally drop with is relative to the item’s base power. High powered or very well scaling unique items are difficult to find with even one legendary potential, and low powered or leveling items are much more likely to have even more than one legendary potential. This is done so that most unique items have more similar overall power potential.
GameGuide.LegendaryItems_Paragraph_8 :: <link="corrupted items">Corrupted items</link> cannot be used to create Legendary Items.

GameGuide.RunesandGlyphs_Title :: Runes and Glyphs
GameGuide.RunesandGlyphs_Paragraph_0 :: Runes are modifier items that can have various effects on the item they are applied to.<br>
GameGuide.RunesandGlyphs_Paragraph_2 :: <#FFE6A5>Rune of Shattering:</color> Destroys an item, creating Shards from its affixes. The number of Shards created is random for each affix, equal to the tier of the affix at maximum and 1 at minimum.
GameGuide.RunesandGlyphs_Paragraph_3 :: <#FFE6A5>Rune of Refinement:</color> Rerolls the values for each affix but does not change their tiers. Each affix is rolled individually, so some may have better values than others.
GameGuide.RunesandGlyphs_Paragraph_4 :: <#FFE6A5>Rune of Removal:</color> Removes a random affix on an item, returning a number of shards equal to its tier.
GameGuide.RunesandGlyphs_Paragraph_5 :: <#FFE6A5>Rune of Discovery:</color> Adds random tier 1 affixes to all empty affix slots on an item. Has an increased chance of rolling rarer affixes. Does not cost Forging Potential, but cannot be applied to items with zero Forging Potential.<br>
GameGuide.RunesandGlyphs_Paragraph_6 :: <#FFE6A5>Rune of Shaping:</color> Rerolls all of the implicits on an item.
GameGuide.RunesandGlyphs_Paragraph_7 :: <#FFE6A5>Rune of Ascendance:</color> Changes the item into a unique or set item of the same item type. Using this on a Silver Ring could turn it into any unique ring, including ones that are not Silver Rings. However, the Silver Ring cannot be turned into a Helmet. None of the other properties of the base item are retained. Rune of Ascendance cannot create unique or set items that are exclusive drops. There are many unique and set items that are only obtainable from bosses, for example.
GameGuide.RunesandGlyphs_Paragraph_8 :: <#FFE6A5>Rune of Creation:</color> Duplicates the item, but reduces the Forging Potential of both the original and the copy to 0.
GameGuide.RunesandGlyphs_Paragraph_9 :: <#FFE6A5>Rune of Havoc:</color> Shuffles the tiers of the non-sealed affixes on an exalted item with 4 non-sealed affixes. For example, an item with tier 2 strength, tier 7 dexterity, tier 3 health and tier 2 endurance might be changed to have tier 7 strength, tier 3 dexterity, tier 2 health, and tier 2 endurance.
GameGuide.RunesandGlyphs_Paragraph_10 :: <#FFE6A5>Rune of Redemption:</color> Randomly changes the exalted affixes on an item into different affixes. Does not change the tiers of the affixes or the rolls within the tiers.
GameGuide.RunesandGlyphs_Paragraph_11 :: <#FFE6A5>Rune of Weaving:</color> Uses a random amount of the remaining <link="weaver''s will">Weaver''s Will</link> or Weaver''s Touch on an item to add or improve affixes as if you had slain many enemies with the item equipped.
GameGuide.RunesandGlyphs_Paragraph_12 :: <#FFE6A5>Rune of Evolution:</color> Evolves an exalted item into a <link="primordial items">Primordial</link> exalted item, upgrading a random tier 7 affix on the item into a tier 8 affix, massively increasing the affix’s power and moving it to its own second sealed affix slot. /  / <#FFE6A5>Rune of Corruption:</color> Rune of Corruption: <link="corrupted items">Corrupts</link> an item, changing it unpredictably and preventing you from being able to do any further crafting on it. The item may no longer be usable after it is corrupted. Removes all Forging Potential, Legendary Potential, Weaver’s Will, and Weaver’s Touch.
GameGuide.RunesandGlyphs_Paragraph_13 :: Glyphs are support items that can be used alongside Runes or <link="shards">Shards</link> to affect the outcome of crafting.<br>
GameGuide.RunesandGlyphs_Paragraph_15 :: <#FFE6A5>Glyph of Hope:</color> Modifies the outcome of a craft, granting it a 25% chance to have no Forging Potential cost.
GameGuide.RunesandGlyphs_Paragraph_16 :: <#FFE6A5>Glyph of Chaos:</color> Modifies the outcome of a craft when upgrading an affix. Randomly changes the upgraded affix to a different one that can spawn on that item type. It cannot change a prefix into a suffix or vice versa. <br><br>For example, if you use a fire resistance shard to a raise the tier of a fire resistance suffix the tier would increase as normal and then it would change into a different affix such as cold resistance or increased health.

GameGuide.AilmentMechanics_Title :: Ailment Mechanics
GameGuide.AilmentMechanics_Paragraph_0 :: An ailment is an effect on a target that lasts for a duration. Most ailments deal damage, apply stat debuffs, or both, and most can be applied to the same target multiple times (or "stack").
GameGuide.AilmentMechanics_Paragraph_1 :: Ailments can either be applied by a <link="Hit vs Damage over Time">hit</link>, or by area effects that attempt to apply ailments at a fixed interval.
GameGuide.AilmentMechanics_Paragraph_2 :: Most ailment applications are chance based. However, having an ailment chance greater than 100% allows you to apply multiple stacks at once if that ailment can stack. <br><br>For example, if you have 235% ignite chance with Fireball, each Fireball hit will apply 2 stacks of ignite, with a 35% chance to apply a 3rd stack of ignite.<br>
GameGuide.AilmentMechanics_Paragraph_3 :: Each damaging ailment, such as Bleed or Ignite, has a fixed base damage, but is affected by damage modifiers from the skill tree and attribute scaling of the skill that applies it.
GameGuide.AilmentMechanics_Paragraph_4 :: Even when an ailment is applied by a melee skill or a hit, that does not make it count as melee damage or hit damage and does not cause it to be affected by stats like increased melee damage.
GameGuide.AilmentMechanics_Paragraph_5 :: If a target has the maximum number of stacks for a given ailment, attempting to apply another stack of that ailment will replace the oldest stack.
GameGuide.AilmentMechanics_Paragraph_6 :: The most common ailments are described in other pages within this section. If an ailment isn''t described here, the tooltip (or alt tooltip) of the node or item that grants it should explain the ailment''s functionality.

GameGuide.Terminology_Paragraph_3 :: Capped Resistance: The maximum resistance you can have. Resistance is capped at 75%. <link="penetrations">Penetration</link> applies against your capped resistance, so a hit that penetrates 10% of your Fire Resistance for example would deal the same damage to you if you had 75% Fire Resistance, or 200% Fire Resistance.
GameGuide.Terminology_Paragraph_4 :: Uncapped Resistance: Uncapped resistance is the total amount of resistance you have against a specific damage type, ignoring the cap of 75%. Uncapped resistance can be useful in mitigating the effect of ailments that reduce your resistance, such as <link="shock">Shock</link>, <link="resistance shred">Resistance Shreds</link> or <link="curse">Marked for Death</link>, but does not help against Penetration past the cap of 75%.
GameGuide.Terminology_Paragraph_5 :: Overcapped Resistance: Any resistance above the cap of 75% is considered overcapped resistance. Overcapped resistance is helpful in mitigating debuffs that lower you resistance, but does not mitigate penetrations.
GameGuide.Terminology_Paragraph_6 :: Favor: Currency obtained from slaying enemies and completing quests that can be spent to gain Reputation with <link="item factions">Item Factions</link>.
GameGuide.Terminology_Paragraph_7 :: Reputation: Your experience with an Item Faction. After gaining enough Reputation you’ll Rank up with your aligned Faction.
GameGuide.Terminology_Paragraph_8 :: Rank: Your level with an Item Faction. Higher your Rank with an Item Faction, the better benefits and perks within the Faction. For example, when aligned with Merchant’s Guild higher rank allows you to purchase higher tier items.
GameGuide.Terminology_Paragraph_9 :: Memory Amber: The currency used within the <link="the woven">Woven Faction</link>. It can be used to purchase <link="woven echoes">Woven Echoes</link> or to respec points inside the <link="weaver tree">Weaver Tree</link>.
GameGuide.Terminology_Paragraph_10 :: Tombs of the Erased: Procedurally generated side zones found inside <link="echoes">Echoes</link>. Completing them usually grants a duplicate Echo reward, and is the primary source for collecting Memory Amber to use within the <link="the woven">Woven Faction</link>.
GameGuide.Terminology_Paragraph_11 :: Cemeteries of the Erased: Procedurally generated side zones similar to Tombs that can be found in the <link="monolith introduction">Monolith of Fate</link>. Once a Cemetery is completed, you can interact with the Altar to the Weaver to place Woven Echoes on to your <link="timelines">Timeline''s</link> Echo Web.
GameGuide.Terminology_Paragraph_12 :: The Altar to the Weaver: An object found at the end of Cemeteries of the Erased, which allows you to place Woven Echoes directly to your current Echo Web.
GameGuide.Terminology_Paragraph_13 :: Silken Cocoons: Destructible objects found in Echoes containing a Tomb or Cemetery of the Erased. Once destroyed, they either release a Soul of the Erased to empower a nearby enemy or an enemy called The Incubated Silk. Both are a good source of obtaining Memory Amber.

GameGuide.Dungeons_Title :: Dungeons
GameGuide.Dungeons_Paragraph_0 :: Dungeons are special challenges throughout the game with a special mechanic, a boss and unique rewards for each one. Entering a Dungeon requires a specific Key to that Dungeon, and there are 4 tiers for each Dungeon with increasing difficulty and rewards.

GameGuide.Resonance_Title :: Resonance
GameGuide.Resonance_Paragraph_0 :: Resonances are special items that expand <link="item gifting">Item Gifting</link>, allowing you to gift specific items to players you play with frequently, even if the item didn’t drop while you were playing with them. Resonances are specific to an individual player. For example, a Golden Resonance that resonates with Moxjet200 (can be seen in the tooltip of the Resonance) allows you to gift an item to Moxjet200. 
GameGuide.Resonance_Paragraph_1 :: There are two different types of Resonance that allow you to gift different varieties of items. GameGuide.Resonance_Paragraph_4 :: Gifting an item with Resonance that has any restriction does not remove or change that restriction. For example, an item that requires to be aligned with <link="circle of fortune">Circle of Fortune</link> to be equipped will still have that requirement after it was gifted.
GameGuide.Resonance_Paragraph_5 :: Resonances are stored when you press the “Transfer Materials” button in your inventory, and can be found in your Gifting window. You can open your gifting window by pressing “Gifting” button in your inventory.

Filters exist in a folder that can be opened from the UI, and placing another filter there will make it available to be used in-game, but a filter can also be imported directly from copying it if your clipboard has a valid Loot Filter saved on it.
GameGuide.LootFilter_Paragraph_5 :: When a Loot Filter is enabled, it is possible to temporarily disable it by holding a keybind (X by default).

GameGuide.Experience_Title :: Experience
GameGuide.Experience_Paragraph_1 :: Experience is gained by completing quests, slaying enemies and from other rewards, such as <link="monolith introduction">Monolith</link> Echoes. As you gain experience, your character and skills will <link="levels">level up</link>, allowing you to customize and make your character stronger. The maximum character level in Last Epoch is 100. You can still gain experience at level 100, it just won’t increase character level anymore.
GameGuide.Experience_Paragraph_3 :: A common way to increase the amount of experience gained is by increasing Corruption in Monolith of Fate, but there are other ways to increase experience such as Experience Shrines. Items in Last Epoch do not provide bonuses to experience gained. 
GameGuide.Experience_Paragraph_5 :: Magic enemies provide 50% more experience gained when slain.
GameGuide.Experience_Paragraph_7 :: Rare enemies provide 200% + additional 4% per level, which means that level 100 rare enemies provide 600% more experience when slain.
GameGuide.Experience_Paragraph_9 :: Boss enemies do not have an experience gain multiplier, but have a much higher base experience reward when slain than other enemies.
GameGuide.Experience_Paragraph_10 :: If a lower level player is in a much higher level area, they gain experience from slaying enemies as if the area was up to 10 levels higher than their character. For example, a level 20 character in a level 40 area would gain experience as if the area was level 30.
GameGuide.Experience_Paragraph_11 :: When playing together with other players and there are big level differences, the previous 10 level difference can go down to a minimum of 2, based on the level difference between the characters. For example, if a level 10 character and a level 70 character were playing together in a level 40 area, the level 10 character would gain experience as if the area was level 12, and the level 70 will gain experience from level 40 area as normal.

GameGuide.StashSearching_Title :: Stash Searching
GameGuide.StashSearching_Paragraph_0 :: The stash supports searching for items based on the contents of their tooltip as well as macros, regex, and expressions to compose more specific searches.
GameGuide.StashSearching_Paragraph_1 :: When searching in the Quick View sidebar, your tabs will be filtered to only show those that contain at least one matching item.
GameGuide.StashSearching_Paragraph_2 :: <b>Usage Examples</b> / - T6+ or 1T6+ matches any exalted item (at least 1 affix is tier 6 or higher) / - 2T7 matches double T7 exalted items (at least 2 affixes are tier 7) / - 3T6+&T7 matches triple exalted items where at least 1 affix is T7 (e.g. T7/T7/T7 or T7/T6/T6 would both match) / - WW&PT20+ would match Weaver''s Will items that have 20 or more Weaver''s Will and legendary affix tiers / - LP3+|WW20+ to find high potential uniques / - prefixes1&T7 to find T7 exalts with an open prefix / - T7&boots to find boots with at least 1 T7 affix / - relic&/1[4-6] dexterity/&suffixes1- to find relics with T7 dexterity and at least 1 open suffix.
GameGuide.StashSearching_Paragraph_3 :: <b>Regex</b> / - Wrap your search in / to search using case-insensitive regex / - e.g. /1[4-9] attunement/ to find items with 14-19 attunement
GameGuide.StashSearching_Paragraph_4 :: <b>Macros</b> /  Macros are case-insensitive short hands that match supported properties of items with an optional quantifier e.g. / - LP0 (is unique with no legendary potential) / - T6+ (has tier 6 or higher affix) / - prefixes2 (has 2 prefixes) /  Most macros match a number on the item and are of the form: Macro[number][+/-] /  If a number is provided, the item must exactly match that number. /  If + or - is provided, the item must be greater/less than or equal to that number. For example: /  - WW matches any Weaver’s Will item and the macro matches the amount of Weaver’s Will on the item /  - WW0 matches any Weaver’s Will item with no Weaver’s Will left /  - WW15+ matches any Weaver’s Will item with at least 15 Weaver’s Will
GameGuide.StashSearching_Paragraph_4_A :: Macros can be negated with ! to invert what they match / - !Corrupted matches items that are not corrupted / - !Trade matches items that are not tradable
GameGuide.StashSearching_Paragraph_5 :: <b>Expressions</b> /  Macros and regex can be combined into expressions /  - Combining with | means either the left or right macro must match /  - Combining with & means both the left and right macro must match
GameGuide.StashSearching_Paragraph_6 :: <b>Supported Macros</b> / Item Potential / - LP (is non-WW unique) / - WW (is WW unique or legendary) / - WT (is enchantable idol) / - FP (is forgeable equipment) / - PT (potential tier) matches items that could be used for unique rerolling in the Gauntlet of Strife.
GameGuide.StashSearching_Paragraph_7 :: Item Types / - Set (grants set bonus) / - RealSet (is set item) / - ReforgedSet (is reforged set item) / - Experimental (is boots/gloves/belt) / - WeaverIdol (is a weaver idol) / - OmenIdol (is an omen idol)
GameGuide.StashSearching_Paragraph_7_A :: Rarity / - Normal / - Magic / - Rare / - Exalted / - Unique / - Legendary / - Set
GameGuide.StashSearching_Paragraph_8 :: Class Requirement / - Acolyte / - Mage / - Primalist / - Rogue / - Sentinel
GameGuide.StashSearching_Paragraph_9 :: Equipment Requirements / - Lvl (required level) / - CoF (Circle of Fortune tagged) / - MG (Merchant''s Guild tagged) / - Trade (can be traded)
GameGuide.StashSearching_Paragraph_10 :: Affix Tier / - T (at least 1 affix tier matches) / - Prepending "aff" or "affix" to a regex will match the tier of a specific affix that matches the regex / - e.g. aff/Hybrid Health/5+ or affix/of the Giant/7
GameGuide.StashSearching_Paragraph_11 :: Affix Tier Count / - These work the same as affix tier macros, but instead of matching any affix, they match if at least a certain number of affixes are matching tiers / - Available affix tier counts are 1-5 (1T, 2T, 3T, 4T, 5T)

GameGuide.RageOfMorditas_Title :: Rage of Morditas
GameGuide.RageOfMorditas_Paragraph_0 :: Morditas is free, filled with rage and ready to get his vengeance.
GameGuide.RageOfMorditas_Paragraph_2 :: Interact with the Blood Crystal to begin the Rage of Morditas encounter, a timed hunt where you defeat monsters to provide souls to Morditas. As you slay enemies, Morditas gathers the souls of the fallen and grants you both Bloodrage charges, tokens that you can use to claim rewards at the end of the encounter, and powerful Bloodrage buffs to aid you further in the slaughter. / Your timer goes up as you slay enemies, with diminishing returns the more monsters you kill. As you progress the encounter the timer keeps ticking down, eventually reaching zero. When this occurs, Morditas appears near you to summon an arena where he proceeds to Bloodcraft the slain enemies back to life.
GameGuide.RageOfMorditas_Paragraph_4 :: Successfully defeating the enemies in the arena awards you with some equippable items, and grants access to the Blood-filled Crystal. Inside this Crystal lies the rewards of the ritual you have just performed, yielding powerful rewards and three options to interact with those rewards - Claim an item, or perform Frozen or Bloody Rituals.  /  / Each of these three options requires spending a Bloodrage charge, which are obtained during the first phase of the encounter. /  / Should you run out of Bloodrage charges, you can complete another encounter and find the same set of rewards waiting for you, as the ritual rewards persist through areas and only provide new rewards when claiming a reward or performing rituals.
GameGuide.RageOfMorditas_Paragraph_6 :: In the Ritual reward window you start with five different rewards. You can then select any of these five rewards to either claim it, or to perform a sacrificing ritual on it. Performing a ritual will empower the next set of rewards, yielding better rewards on average. / Claiming the reward consumes one Bloodrage charge and grants you the item. The Ritual rewards then start from the beginning, with another new five options. Any empowerment your ritual had is also removed.
GameGuide.RageOfMorditas_Paragraph_8 :: Performing the Frozen Ritual sacrifices the marked item to reroll and empower the ritual for better rewards. Rewards after using the Frozen Ritual can only include items of the same item type, such as boots, and one of the rewards will be the same subtype as the sacrificed item if possible. Forgeable items are granted a new type of Ice <link="forging potential">Forging Potential</link> that has higher chance to preserve Forging Potential when crafting.
GameGuide.RageOfMorditas_Paragraph_10 :: Performing the Bloody Ritual sacrifices the marked item to reroll and empower the ritual for better rewards. Rewards after using the Bloody Ritual cannot include the same item type, such as boots. Forgeable items are granted a new type of Blood Forging Potential that has higher chance to critically success when crafting.
GameGuide.RageOfMorditas_Paragraph_12 :: If an exclusive Bloodrage <link="rarity">unique</link> item is one of the reward options, it won''t be rerolled to a new item, instead the rituals will have a significant impact on the item. Frozen Ritual rerolls its special affixes, and Bloody Ritual either adds <link="legendary items">Legendary Potential</link> to the item or <link="corrupted items">corrupts</link> it, if it''s not yet corrupted.
GameGuide.RageOfMorditas_Paragraph_14 :: As you defeat more Bloodcrafted enemies, Morditas'' rage grows stronger, and unlocks additional Rage of Morditas encounters through the <link="The Nexus">Nexus</link>, leading all the way to the Circle of Frozen Blood, where you may challenge Morditas himself. / Each of these unique encounters can be scaled in difficulty and rewards through Anger that grows naturally as you complete encounters. You can also lower the Anger to make the encounters easier, but less rewarding.
